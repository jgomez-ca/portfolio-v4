Diagnosing & Fixing Custom Domain Email Delivery Issues

A runbook for finding out why mail from a custom domain bounces or lands in spam, using SPF, DKIM, and DMARC.

Audience
New support specialists, support engineers
Read time
10 minutes
Practice time
15 minutes in a sandbox
Scenario
example.com (fictional)

Outcome and when to use this guide

After this guide you can verify a domain's email DNS records, spot common syntax and alignment mistakes, and resolve bounced or spam-filtered mail.

Use this guide when

  • A customer's mail bounces with an authentication error.
  • Mail from their domain lands in spam.
  • They just changed email providers or DNS hosts.

Escalate instead when

  • Records are correct but delivery still fails.
  • The sending IP appears on a blocklist.
  • The customer asks you to change DNS you cannot access.

How DNS, mail servers, and authentication fit together

A receiving mail server doesn't trust the "From" address on its own. It looks up three kinds of DNS records on the sender's domain to decide whether the message is real.

SPF

Lists which servers may send mail for the domain. Stored as a TXT record on the domain itself.

DKIM

Adds a signature to each message. The public key sits in a TXT record at selector._domainkey.

DMARC

Tells receivers what to do when SPF or DKIM fails, and requires the results to match the visible From domain (alignment). Stored at _dmarc.

Step-by-step diagnostic workflow

Tick each step as you finish it. Your progress is saved in this browser.

  1. Get the failure details

    Ask for the full bounce message or the message headers. Note the sending domain, the error code, and the Authentication-Results line.

  2. Check that each record exists

    Query all three records. Replace selector1 with the selector from the message headers (s= in the DKIM-Signature).

    dig +short TXT example.com
dig +short TXT selector1._domainkey.example.com
dig +short TXT _dmarc.example.com
    Common pitfall: wrong selector

    A typo in the selector returns nothing, which looks like a missing record. Copy the selector from the headers instead of typing it.

  3. Check propagation

    If the customer changed DNS recently, query the authoritative name servers directly and compare with a public resolver.

    dig +short NS example.com
dig +short TXT example.com @ns1.example-dns.net
dig +short TXT example.com @1.1.1.1

    If the answers differ, wait for the record's TTL to pass, then test again.

  4. Validate syntax

    Paste each record into the checker below. Look for these errors first.

    Common pitfall: multiple SPF records

    A domain can have only one SPF record. If a second provider told the customer to "add" a record, merge the include: entries into one.

    Common pitfall: too many lookups

    SPF allows 10 DNS lookups. Each include, a, mx, and redirect counts.

  5. Verify alignment

    DMARC passes only if SPF or DKIM passes and its domain matches the visible From domain. Compare the From domain to the d= value in the DKIM signature and the Return-Path domain.

    Common pitfall: overlooked subdomains

    Mail sent from news.example.com needs its own SPF and DKIM setup unless the DMARC policy and alignment mode cover it. Check which exact domain sends.

  6. Apply the fix, then confirm

    Ask the customer to update the record, wait for the TTL, then send a test message to a mailbox you can read. Open the headers and confirm SPF, DKIM, and DMARC all show pass.

  7. Escalate or close

    Escalation criteria

    Escalate if all three records pass but mail is still rejected, the sending IP is blocklisted, DKIM fails with a correct record (possible key rotation issue at the provider), or the customer reports a suspected spoofing attack.

    Otherwise, document the cause and the fix in the ticket and close it.
